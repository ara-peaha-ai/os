Five distinct groups are converging on this opportunity at the same time.

**Paraguay as a destination for international capital.** Territorial tax system — only income generated inside the country is taxed locally. No capital gains tax on foreign-source income. A direct residency path that takes a few months. Growing foreign investment in construction, land, and business relocation. The market is attracting capital from Europe, North America, and within Latin America simultaneously.

**Construction is expanding fast.** Registered real estate transactions in Paraguay reached USD 1,856M in 2024. New residential and corporate building construction grew 5.9% year-on-year. The 2025 projection is approximately USD 1,900M, based on the 2024 trend. Source: BCP / official 2024 data.

**Bitcoiners with purchasing power ready to deploy.** A segment of early adopters now holds significant on-chain wealth and is actively looking to convert it into real assets. Real estate is the natural destination. The execution problem is exactly what Coin2Local solves: the seller doesn't accept Bitcoin, the buyer doesn't want full KYC liquidation through an exchange.

**European long-term on-chain holders diversifying.** Holders accumulating since 2015–2018 are increasingly looking at physical assets outside their home jurisdiction. Paraguay offers territorial taxation, no capital gains on foreign-source income, and property prices well below comparable European markets. The residency process is direct.

**Latin American operators already moving in stablecoins.** Medium-to-high-profit businesses across Latin America already use USDT and USDC for cross-border transactions. Settling a real estate payment in Paraguay is a natural extension — same rails, same logic, larger ticket.

These five groups meet at the same market. Coin2Local handles the payment execution.

## Construction companies in Paraguay

The primary focus of the real estate vertical is Paraguayan construction companies.

They sit at the intersection of all five profiles above. They receive large international payments — often from the same European and Latin American buyer segments described — and they operate entirely in local fiat for suppliers, payroll, and permits. They have no infrastructure to accept Bitcoin or stablecoin payments directly, and their local banking relationships don't solve the inbound international payment problem. The gap is structural, not accidental.

## Document checklist

#### For bank settlement in Paraguay

- Passport or valid government-issued ID for each shareholder / partner / beneficial owner.
- Recent personal proof of address for each shareholder / partner / beneficial owner, usually issued within the last 3 months, such as a utility bill, bank statement, residence certificate, or equivalent local document.
- Recent business proof of address for the company, usually issued within the last 3 months, such as a utility bill, bank statement, office lease agreement, property certificate, or equivalent document issued in the company's name.
- Company registration certificate or official proof of incorporation issued by the relevant corporate registry or public authority.
- Articles of incorporation, company bylaws, statutes, operating agreement, or equivalent constitutional documents.
- Shareholder register, cap table, stock ledger, members register, or equivalent corporate document showing the ownership structure, shareholders / members, and percentage or number of shares held by each one.

#### For cash settlement

No documentation is typically required. Specific requirements are confirmed case by case.
